The idea

The tyranny of software

I was running an animation program when I noticed something that kept me up at night: very talented students were handing in work that all looked alike. It was not a shortage of ideas. The software had already made most of the decisions before they opened it.

This does not only happen to art students. It happens in any team, classroom or organization that adopts a tool and slowly starts producing whatever that tool makes easy. The usual answer is to add points, badges and leaderboards. That usually makes it worse: people start optimizing the score instead of the work.

My work proposes the opposite move: keep the game, change who it serves. In my field that has a name — postgamification — and my doctoral thesis at UNAM is where I set it out. In practice it comes down to something simple: designing so that people get to decide again.

Work & projects

Theory, made object

MAPA H.06

An encyclopedia you play. It fits in a single file, works with no internet and makes zero network calls: 673 interlinked entries in Spanish and English, with seven short games around them. Produced for Centro de Cultura Digital within its electronic-literature program, aimed above all at teenagers.

Centro de Cultura Digital · CC BY 4.0 + MIT · in production

EL TARJETERO

Emotional first aid in an HTML file that fits in a message: 27 cards, 1,307 sourced lines, 505 reference entries and 180 DOIs. No account, no telemetry, no streaks. It states in writing what the evidence does not support. A pocket edition for children also exists.

Research prototype · v1 · 2026

NIDOS IMPROPIOS

Painting: acrylic on paper and reflective surface, with UV-reactive layers. Made within Proyecto Chata, my studio practice with Ramsés Olaya. Three exhibitions during 2026.
