The idea

The tyranny of software

I was running an animation program when I noticed something that kept me up at night: very talented students were handing in work that all looked alike. It was not a shortage of ideas. The software had already made most of the decisions before they opened it.

This does not only happen to art students. It happens in any team or organization that adopts a tool and slowly starts producing whatever that tool makes easy. The usual answer is to add points, badges and leaderboards. That usually makes it worse: people optimize the score instead of the work.

My work proposes the opposite move: keep the game, change who it serves. In my field that has a name — postgamification — and my doctoral thesis at UNAM is where I set it out.
